**Paired changes in experimental decision accuracy after adding score-profile information.** Panels a,b show new molecules with known proteins; c,d show new proteins with known molecules. Left: agreement with the interval-identified direction of D, the difference between two molecular changes at distinct target assays; predicted ties receive half credit. Right: the joint choice is correct only when both predicted molecular changes have the observed opposite directions. Each point is the indicated arm minus the global-level-plus-chemistry baseline on common evaluated rectangles, in percentage points (pp); horizontal lines are the 2.5th–97.5th percentiles of 400 paired chemical-cluster resampling draws with target-assay contexts fixed. Actual assay pairs receive equal weight in the all-source operational IC50 contrast domain. Source variants retain the original identity weighting; source records and bounds are not pooled into a thermodynamic selectivity estimate. These intervals preserve molecule dependencies and are not rectangle-binomial intervals or a declared success threshold. The full-profile arm adds the full centered profile. The matching-target residual arm adds a source-matched column residual. All displayed arms are fitted readouts on learned-common support. The selected-column residual arm uses training interval-order association for known proteins and the sequence-nearest available column for held-out proteins, without held-out labels. The latter can coincide with the matching-target residual when the nearest source column is the native match; equal values are not independent evidence. Exact plotted estimates, paired intervals, baseline accuracies, all weighting/domain sensitivities, support and within-assay absolute accuracy are supplied as CSV alongside this figure. The support table gives scored experimental identities, actual assay pairs and rectangles separately; rectangle counts are not independent sample sizes. No best or worst condition was selected.
